# Investor / Startup Pitch Decks

Module drafted 2026-10-09 from research dossier (Track B, Dossier 4). Purpose: raise capital. Most decks are now read via link without the founder present, so self-explanatory is mandatory, not optional.

## 1. Three canons, one reconciled spine

**Sequoia "Writing a Business Plan" (15–20 slides):** company purpose as one declarative sentence → problem → solution ("eureka moment") → WHY NOW → market (TAM top-down + SAM bottom-up) → competition/alternatives → product → business model → team → financials (P&L, cash flow, cap table, THE DEAL).

**Kawasaki 10/20/30:** 10 slides, 20 minutes, 30pt minimum font. Treat as a psychological filter (brevity forces discipline), NOT formatting law — cramming 40 slides' content into 10 is a documented failure mode.

**YC seed (10–12 slides):** title → problem → solution → market → traction → team → ask. Lead with whatever is strongest; include a non-obvious-insight slide; traction must be dated and specific (never smoothed hockey sticks); team = founders, not advisors; ask = amount + what it buys.

**Reconciliation:** shared spine Problem → Solution → Market → Model → Competition → Team. Target **10–12 core slides + appendix**, hard ceiling ~20 (DocSend: successful seed decks average 19.2 pages; engagement drops after 20).

- [ ] Core deck is 10–12 slides with an appendix; it never exceeds ~20 slides total.
- [ ] The spine (problem → solution → market → model → competition → team) is present in order; each element appears exactly once.
- [ ] The strongest card leads (YC rule): if traction is the story, traction opens.
- [ ] The appendix carries everything non-core (extra product detail, cohort tables, cap table, backup slides).

## 2. Three genuinely disputed points

1. **Team placement:** Sequoia puts team 9th; YC/DocSend data argue early — Team is the #2 most-viewed section. Default: team early or with a strong anchor, never buried at the end.
2. **Why-now:** skipped by ~75% of decks, yet it is a Sequoia signature slide. Include it; most founders skip it, which is exactly why it stands out.
3. **Financials:** present in only 58% of successful seed decks, but most-viewed when present. Include a tight financials frame (unit economics, burn), not detailed seed financials.

- [ ] Team is not buried at the end; why-now has a slide even though ~75% of decks skip it; financials are tight, not a spreadsheet.

## 3. Canonical slide list (checklist)

- [ ] Cover: company purpose as one declarative sentence.
- [ ] Problem.
- [ ] Solution / "eureka moment."
- [ ] Why now.
- [ ] Market: TAM top-down + SAM bottom-up (bottom-up TAM, not vanity top-down).
- [ ] Competition / alternatives (never "we have no competitors").
- [ ] Product.
- [ ] Business model.
- [ ] Traction: dated, specific (no smoothed hockey sticks, no undated claims).
- [ ] Team: founders, not advisors.
- [ ] Ask: amount + what it buys. (No deal terms in the deck.)

## 4. Design conventions

- [ ] One idea per slide; one point per graphic.
- [ ] Legible: 30pt minimum, simple typeface, dark on white.
- [ ] Headlines declarative, not cute.
- [ ] Line graphs for growth; bottom-up TAM graphics.
- [ ] A stranger must understand each slide at a glance (the link-reading test).
- [ ] DocSend data to respect: average view time is only 3:44; Team appears in 100% of successful decks; 12% of views are mobile (test on a phone screen).

## 5. Common mistakes (fail the build if any survive)

- [ ] Not too long or crammed.
- [ ] Strongest card not buried (no Team-at-the-end, no skipped why-now).
- [ ] No feature-list opening.
- [ ] No deal terms in the deck.
- [ ] No "we have no competitors."
- [ ] No vanity top-down TAMs (trillion-dollar global market served to no one).
- [ ] No detailed seed financials.
- [ ] Indian investor blacklist (YourStory survey): no motivational quotes, no celebrity name-drops, no advisor lists, no tech-stack deep dives, no valuation/exit/M&A plans.
- [ ] No typos (an explicit blacklist item).
- [ ] No fake traction: no undated hockey sticks, no MoUs-as-traction.

## 6. India notes

- **No structural difference at VC level.** Indian VCs (Sequoia India/Peak XV, YourStory survey) teach the identical global format. The user's "not common in India" observation does NOT hold here.
- [ ] The metric bar is what differs: MRR/ARR, gross margin, cohort retention, CAC payback, burn multiple. Vanity metrics (downloads, GMV, MoUs) "carry little weight" (LegalSuvidha 2026).
- [ ] No valuation in the outbound deck (Rule 11UA works when asked).
- Indian formats to know: ISFM 2026 (Problem → Solution → Market → Model → GTM → Traction → Competition → Team → Financials → Ask + 3-min video); IIT Bombay Eureka! (rigorous unit economics; the "use of funds" slide is often decisive).

## 7. Field notes (Track C, clearly labeled)

- Real early-stage decks ran **7–10 slides** — shorter than the canon; they are product-demo-heavy and skip the financial machinery the canon prescribes.
- DermoAI case: 4 of 7 slides were feature walkthrough, with NO market, team, ask, or financials slides.
- Mixed title phrasing observed (labels + assertions + imperatives); median 66–71 words/slide; image-heavy with zero native charts.
- Famous decks (Airbnb/Uber) are PDF-only: no famous .pptx was obtainable.
