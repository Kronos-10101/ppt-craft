# Academic / class presentation — reference module

**Purpose:** coursework seminars, project presentations, classroom teaching decks. Two distinct genres: the **student talk** (defends work under questioning) vs the **instructor lecture** (teaches). Never build one as the other (dossier dated 2026-10-09).

## Canonical structure (student talk)

- [ ] **Title slide:** name, course, guide — all three, no exceptions.
- [ ] **Motivation FIRST** — mandatory, even before the agenda (Hagen). Why should anyone care?
- [ ] **Agenda** — **omit for 10–15 min talks**; there is no room for a roadmap slide.
- [ ] **Objectives:** what this talk will establish, stated plainly.
- [ ] **Background, minimal:** just enough for the audience to follow the work — not a textbook chapter.
- [ ] **Literature review: gaps, not dumps.** What is missing in prior work, not everything ever written.
- [ ] **Methodology:** key procedures **+ why** this design fits the question.
- [ ] **Results:** 2–3 key figures, figure-led, one finding per slide.
- [ ] **Discussion / limitations:** proactive, not defensive. Own the weak spots before the questions do.
- [ ] **Conclusion + future work** → **References** → **SUMMARY slide — never a bare "Thank you"** (Hagen: "completely useless") → Q&A.

## Budget

- [ ] **~1 min per slide.** UNE: 5–6 slides per 10 min. York: 7–8 slides per 12 min.
- [ ] Plan the talk to finish with time for questions; a talk that eats the Q&A window reads as unprepared.
- [ ] For a 10–15 min talk, the agenda slide is the first thing to cut — that slot is one minute of content lost.

## Assertion–evidence model (Alley — most evidence-backed design for this genre)

- [ ] Sentence-assertion headline, ≤2 lines, 28pt bold sans-serif: the headline IS the takeaway, not the topic.
- [ ] Visual evidence instead of bullets: the figure/table/photo makes the case, ≤~20 words of body text.
- [ ] Lists capped at 2–4 items; whitespace (roughly ½ inch) under the headline — the eye needs room.
- [ ] Duarte: one idea per slide; headline = takeaway; visuals support, never decorate.

## Typography and readability

- [ ] Titles 38–48pt (APSU); body **≥24pt**. If it cannot be read from the back row, it is decoration.
- [ ] ≤6 lines per slide, ≤7 words per line.
- [ ] ~3 colors; high contrast; color-blind-safe where figures carry meaning.
- [ ] Cite borrowed material: short-form attribution on the slide (author, year), full list at the end.
- [ ] Accessibility: sufficient contrast, alt-text on figures, logical reading order for screen readers.

## Teaching decks differ (instructor genre)

- [ ] Slides are **cues, not the lecture**: the explanation lives in the speaker, not on the screen.
- [ ] Signpost and chunk: section headers that tell students where they are in the lesson.
- [ ] Label **exam-essential vs non-essential** content explicitly.
- [ ] Teaching decks may carry **more text as study aids** — a deliberate exception to the density rules above, because students revise from them after class.

## Common mistakes

- [ ] Text walls — the #1 failure in this genre.
- [ ] Reading slides aloud — the audience ends up "listening to two people at once".
- [ ] Revealing everything at once instead of pacing reveals to the argument.
- [ ] Decoration and distraction: visuals that do not support the claim they sit next to.
- [ ] Slides doing the speaker's job: if the slide needs no speaker, it is a document, not a talk.
- [ ] No story: method → results → conclusion with no motivation or narrative arc.
- [ ] Ending on a bare "Thank you" slide instead of a summary — the last thing the audience sees must be content.

## Delivery checklist

- [ ] Every figure is explained, not just shown: what it is, what it shows, why it matters.
- [ ] Limitations are stated in the talk, not discovered in Q&A.
- [ ] Ends on the summary slide, held through Q&A as the anchor.

## India notes (sourced — study/editorial findings, not universal fact)

- [ ] **Raut & Gupta** (Maharashtra medical college study): 51.6% of students preferred chalk-and-board; 78.7% of teachers preferred PPT; students could not match note-taking pace to slide changes.
- [ ] **Shinde & Patil (2015):** student verbatim — "professors just read the stuff from their slides without elaborating"; recommendation: use PPT for pictures/videos only.
- [ ] **VTU pattern:** 4 mandatory PowerPoint seminars as graded deliverables (hard + soft copy submitted) — the PPT is an institutional artifact, not just a talk aid.
- [ ] The Hindu (2014): 24–44pt type, 6 lines max — matches global norms.
- [ ] **Not verified (2026-10-09):** no pan-India text-density study found; no AICTE/UGC slide standard found. Do not generalize the above findings beyond their sources.

## FIELD NOTES (Track C — observed, not prescribed)

Empirical parse of 3 real class decks:

- **11–13 slides**, tightly clustered across all three decks.
- Almost entirely **topic labels** ("Data Preprocessing", "Classification – Decision Tree") — zero assertion headlines.
- Very sparse text: median 1–44 words/slide; one deck's content was entirely image-embedded.
- **Zero native charts**; images carry the figures.
- One deck kept its real content in speaker notes — notes on 13/13 slides.
- **No references/bibliography in any class deck**; no formal motivation slide; results compressed.
- "Ethical Considerations" / "Practical Recommendations" appear as course-rubric artifacts, not narrative choices.
